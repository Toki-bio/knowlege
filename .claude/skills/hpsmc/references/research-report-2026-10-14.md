# hPSMC dates the end of gene flow, not the split

hPSMC (hybrid or pseudo-diploid PSMC) builds a fake F1 diploid from one haploidized genome per lineage and reads the lineages' separation from the point where inferred Ne rises sharply. It was published as Cahill, Soares, Green and Shapiro 2016, Phil Trans R Soc B 371(1699):20150138 ([PMC record](https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/efetch.fcgi?db=pmc&id=PMC4920339&retmode=xml)). It was validated on simulations and on great apes and *Ursus* bears. No source I found tests hPSMC on human population pairs. Its reliable window is untested above 5 Ma, and no published maximum divergence exists. For *Sicista betulina* vs *S. trizona*, the hPSMC result of 0.7-0.85 Myr and the BPP result of 1.19 Myr do not conflict with each other. hPSMC is expected to run younger than a no-gene-flow multispecies-coalescent (MSC) estimate if any post-split gene flow occurred, and the 0.6-0.7 ratio fits that direction. The "published 2.6 Mya" is a fixed calibration prior on a different node (subtilis group vs betulina group), not a betulina-vs-trizona estimate, so it cannot be used to judge either method. The analysis is not yet shown to be correct: the mutation rate and generation time are placeholders that scale every date, and several data-quality issues (ONT errors, ROH, possible WGA, X chromosome, cross-reference mapping) are unquantified. Source-quality labels used below: **[direct]** read in full text by the note-taker; **[summary]** from a tool-generated summary only; **[inference]** the note-taker's reasoning; **[unverified]** not confirmed.

## What hPSMC is, and what it was validated on

Each individual is haploidized (one base call per site, `samtools mpileup -q30 -Q60` piped into `pu2fa`, per the README). The two haploid sequences are merged with `psmcfa_from_2_fastas.py -b10 -m5`, and PSMC is run with `-N25 -t15 -r5 -p "4+25*2+4+6"` ([hPSMC README](https://github.com/jacahill/hPSMC)) **[direct]**. A difference between the two calls is coded as a heterozygote, so the pseudo-diploid "heterozygosity" is pairwise divergence between the two individuals **[inference]**. Because the two alleles can coalesce only in the ancestral population, Ne rises toward very large values after the split ([SMC review, PMC6972798](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6972798/)) **[direct]**. The idea predates Cahill: Li and Durbin 2011 combined the X chromosomes of a Yoruba and a non-African male into a pseudo-diploid ([Li and Durbin 2011](https://reich.hms.harvard.edu/sites/reich.hms.harvard.edu/files/inline-files/2011_Li_Nature_0.pdf)) **[direct]**.

The time is not read by eye. The README has the user estimate pre-divergence Ne, simulate clean splits with ms (`hPSMC_quantify_split_time.py`, no post-split migration), keep simulations whose curve crosses the data between 1.5x and 10x pre-divergence Ne, and report the narrowest range of inconsistent simulations around them ([README](https://github.com/jacahill/hPSMC)) **[direct]**. The abstract states that the method works "even without high coverage or phased input data", that it confirmed most proposed ape and *Ursus* dates, and that it suggests up to 1 Myr of continued chimpanzee-bonobo gene flow ([PMC abstract](https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/efetch.fcgi?db=pmc&id=PMC4920339&retmode=xml)) **[direct]**.

The notes disagree on whether the Cahill full text was read. The method and limits notes could not open it. The Sicista note says it retrieved the PMC text directly. Every Cahill detail below beyond the abstract is therefore carried as **[summary]**, and none of it should be quoted verbatim without checking the PDF. These details are: simulations (ms, 200 Mb) with splits from 100 ka to 5 Ma, pre-divergence Ne 1,000-50,000, and 0.1-1,000 migrants per generation at a 1 Ma split; apes (chimp-bonobo 300-450 ka, human vs chimp/bonobo about 1.75-3.75 Ma, orangutan lineage about 7.5-13 Ma, the latter outside the simulated range); and bears (polar vs brown within 200 ka, which the authors say conflicts with fossils and attribute to recent admixture) ([Royal Society page](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/doi/10.1098/rstb.2015.0138)). No accuracy or bias figure from the paper is verified, and none is quoted here.

Two corrections to the original recollection stand. The paper is in Phil Trans B, not Mol Biol Evol or Mol Ecol. It covers great apes and *Ursus* bears, not only polar vs brown bear.

## Human populations: only a precursor, no direct test

I found no paper applying hPSMC itself to human population pairs or to human vs archaic pairs. This is absence of evidence from a limited search of about 25 calls; "cited by" lists for Cahill 2016 were not opened **[unverified negative]**. The only human data in Cahill is one human genome treated as one of five great-ape taxa, in deep splits **[summary]**. The nearest human analogues are not hPSMC:

- Li and Durbin 2011, Yoruba vs non-African male X pseudo-diploid: Ne was low until about 20 ka and effectively infinite more recently. They read this as gene exchange until 20-40 ka rather than a clean split near 60 ka. A simulated clean split at 60 ka gave a different curve ([Li and Durbin 2011](https://reich.hms.harvard.edu/sites/reich.hms.harvard.edu/files/inline-files/2011_Li_Nature_0.pdf)) **[direct]**. The humans note could not open this paper, but the method note read it. This is the closest thing to a human hPSMC test, and it used X chromosomes with X-specific scaling.
- Prüfer et al. 2014 used a two-chromosome PSMC for modern human vs Neandertal/Denisovan splits (553-589 ka at mu 0.5e-9 per bp per year) ([DASH copy](https://dash.harvard.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/7312037d-3fde-6bd4-e053-0100007fdf3b/content)) **[summary]**. Whether it equals hPSMC is my inference only.
- MSMC and MSMC2 cross-coalescence curves show human separation as gradual, not clean. African vs non-African curves decline slowly from before 200 ka to about 40 ka, and clean-split simulations at 50, 100 and 150 ka did not reproduce them ([Schiffels and Durbin 2014](https://stephanschiffels.de/pdfs/Schiffels2014-xn.pdf)) **[summary]**. Papuan split times are incompatible with a simple bifurcating tree ([Pagani et al. 2016](https://dash.harvard.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/7312037e-41e1-6bd4-e053-0100007fdf3b/content)) **[summary]**.

My prediction, untested **[inference]**, is that an hPSMC run on these human pairs would give the end of substantial gene flow, not the onset of divergence. I found no benchmark of any kind that includes hPSMC; the human split-time comparisons are each method's own simulations.

## Limits in time and distance: a measured floor, an untested ceiling

No published numeric ceiling exists for divergence time, per-bp divergence, or heterozygous sites per window **[direct, absence in sources read]**. The only simulated range is 100 ka to 5 Ma **[summary]**. Applications go far beyond it. Sea turtles gave gene-flow cessation at 7-14 Ma, with 10-bp bins ([Mol Ecol 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9292604)) **[direct]**. Birds-of-paradise gave about 10 Ma or more ([Evol Lett 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11424083)) **[direct]**. Those authors report the values as end of gene flow, far younger than phylogenetic splits, and no simulation validates them. Treat them as "coalescence signal in the oldest intervals", not demonstrated accuracy **[inference]**. The PSMC family shares a deep-time ceiling: MSMC2 reported to 3 Myr and cobraa about 5 Ma ([Wang et al. 2020](https://journals.plos.org/plosgenetics/article?id=10.1371/journal.pgen.1008552); [cobraa README](https://github.com/trevorcousins/cobraa)) **[direct, README/paper statements]**.

Five mechanisms bound the usable range.

- **Bin saturation.** PSMC codes 100-bp bins as heterozygous or not. The cobraa README warns that theta above 0.01 makes the default 100-bp bin inappropriate and suggests -b 5 or 10 at theta 0.05 **[direct]**. By my calculation the fraction of heterozygous 100-bp bins is 1-(1-d)^100, about 63% at d = 1% and about 87% at 2%. No source gives an hPSMC cutoff **[inference]**. Cahill's simulations and the turtle and bird studies used 10-bp bins.
- **Few recombination events at depth.** Li and Durbin report small variance between about 20 kyr and 3 Myr in human-scaled simulations, and large variance outside it. Time runs in units of 2N0 with Tmax = 15, so the ceiling in years scales with Ne and mu ([Li and Durbin 2011](https://reich.hms.harvard.edu/sites/reich.hms.harvard.edu/files/inline-files/2011_Li_Nature_0.pdf)) **[direct]** **[inference for the Ne scaling]**.
- **Post-split gene flow.** At 1 or more migrants per generation (1 Ma split) no divergence signal appeared; at 0.1 it appeared but less precisely **[summary]**. The SMC review says hPSMC gives "only a maximum bound" and is better used to date cessation of gene flow ([PMC6972798](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6972798/)) **[direct]**. A Delphinoidea paper reports sensitivity to as few as 1/10,000 migrants per generation; the attribution of those simulations to Cahill is unverified **[summary]**.
- **Ancestral Ne.** Pre-divergence Ne above about 10,000 made populations appear to diverge earlier than the truth in haploidized simulations **[summary]**. Reviewers of the kiwi paper note that hPSMC cannot distinguish incomplete lineage sorting (ILS) from gene flow, and that selection and hidden structure probably bias it. This is reviewer opinion, not a test ([Kiwi, PLoS One 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9550048)).
- **Time scaling.** Dates scale with the assumed mu and generation time. Cahill's own text says a wrong mu skews estimates proportionally **[summary/unverified provenance, see above]**.

Phylogenetic distance matters mainly through mapping. Studies of PSMC (not hPSMC) show Ne estimates fall as the reference gets more distant. Effects appeared beyond about 0.14% reference difference in a cetacean and kiwi study ([Prasad et al. bioRxiv 2021](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2021.03.03.433733.full.pdf)) **[direct as preprint]**. Salmonid mapping fell below 70% at 1.45-1.72% divergence ([RADseq study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7391306)). Two hPSMC applications that mapped to two references (sea turtles) saw no difference, and the kiwi authors cite prior work that hPSMC is not influenced by reference. That prior work is not identified **[unverified]**. No source gives a "1-5% tolerance"; I could not find that figure **[unverified]**. No test of reference bias on the hPSMC spike exists.

## Sicista betulina vs S. trizona: consistent with expectations, not yet validated

**The 2.6 Ma figure.** In Deng et al. 2025 (Zoosyst Evol 101(3):1197-1211) it is a BEAST calibration, "the divergence between Sicista subtilis and S. betulina, set at approximately 2.60 Ma (mean = 2.6, standard deviation = 0.1)", justified by the fossil record (Kowalski 2001) ([Deng et al. PDF](https://zse.pensoft.net/article/155510/download/pdf/)) **[direct]**. Zhu et al. 2025 (Zool Res 46(4):921-938) fix the same value as a lognormal prior, "subtilis group vs. betulina group split: 2.2-3.1 Ma (from Lebedev et al., 2019)" ([Zhu et al.](https://www.zoores.ac.cn/en/article/pdf/preview/10.24272/j.issn.2095-8137.2025.013.pdf)) **[summary]**. The two papers give different provenance (Kowalski vs "Lebedev 2019"), and I could not open or identify either source. Neither paper names *S. trizona* at that node. *S. trizona* was split from *S. subtilis* only in 2016 (Cserkesz et al., Mammal Review 46:114-130) ([MDD](https://mammaldiversity.org/taxon/1002010)) **[direct as database]**. A fixed prior carries no information from the data. I found no published, data-derived betulina-vs-trizona divergence with a confidence interval. So the 2.6 Ma figure is neither a benchmark nor evidence against 0.7-1.19 Myr.

**Is hPSMC applied correctly?** The notes support the points below.

| Issue | Status |
|---|---|
| Direction of difference from BPP | An hPSMC date younger than a no-gene-flow MSC date is the direction Cahill's gene-flow result predicts ([Cahill 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4920339)) **[summary]**. The BPP side is general knowledge, unsourced in the notes. Opposite-sign effects (large ancestral Ne, artefact differences) are possible, so gene flow is not demonstrated. |
| Placeholder mu and generation time | 5.4e-9 per generation is the C57BL/6 mutation-accumulation value ([Uchimura et al. 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4509997)) **[direct]**. No dipodid mutation rate or generation time was found. Dates scale as 1/mu and linearly with generation time. If the real generation time is 1.5-2 yr, 0.7-0.85 Myr becomes about 1.05-1.7 Myr **[inference]**. A 1-2 yr generation is my inference from ~7 months of hibernation and one litter per year, not a sourced figure. The hPSMC/BPP ratio holds only if both used the same mu and generation time. |
| Saturation | At the placeholder mu and 1 yr generations, a 0.8 Myr split gives d of roughly 0.9% from the split alone (2·mu·T), so about 58% of default 100-bp bins would be heterozygous **[my calculation, ignores ancestral coalescence]**. Confirm that -b10 was used. |
| Simulation bracketing | The README requires simulating clean splits at the user's own Ne, mu and generation time. The notes do not say this was done. Pre-divergence Ne above 10,000 would bias older **[summary]**. |
| X chromosome | Both animals are male, so X is hemizygous; heterozygote calls there are errors, and the X Ne is 3/4 of autosomal in the neutral expectation. Li and Durbin corrected X specifically. Exclusion is my inference; no source gives an hPSMC rule. The betulina assembly (1,144 contigs) needs X-contig identification first. |
| ONT assembly | No source on ONT in PSMC/hPSMC. Indel-driven misalignment adds spurious differences, which would push the split older **[inference]**. |
| ROH (~30% of betulina, as stated by the user) | Within-individual ROH does not remove cross-individual differences **[inference]**. It could affect the pre-divergence Ne plateau that anchors the 1.5x-10x window. No source quantifies this. |
| WGA | The BGE project page describes planned PacBio HiFi at about 40x and does not mention WGA ([BGE](https://bge.biodiversitygenomics.eu/?p=6635)). This conflicts with the stated WGA, and the page predates sequencing. Effects of WGA on hPSMC are unquantified and the net direction is uncertain **[inference]**. |
| Short contigs | PSMC was consistent down to 100-kb scaffolds and lost resolution at 10 kb, in human and simulated data ([bioRxiv 382036](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/382036.full.pdf)). Applicability to contig-level assemblies is untested. |
| Cross-reference mapping | Mapping both to the trizona reference makes betulina mismatches include divergence-driven mapping error **[inference]**. |
| Assembly input | The README workflow is read-based (mpileup). How haploid calls were derived from assemblies is not covered by the notes. |

The mitogenome paper on *S. betulina* shows how rate-sensitive group dating is: its TMRCA for betulina + strandi (527 ka under the fast rate) rises roughly threefold under the slow rate ([PMC9022092](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9022092/)) **[direct]**. It reports tentative betulina-strandi introgression about 100 ka. That concerns strandi, not trizona. No gene-flow evidence specific to betulina and trizona was found.

## Next steps for the Sicista analysis

1. Recompute both dates with the same documented mu and generation time. Report sensitivity over mu 3e-9 to 1e-8 per generation and generation time 1-2 yr. Do not present a single placeholder-based date.
2. Confirm 10-bp bins (-b10) in hPSMC and test -p variants (for example `2+2+25*2+4+6`) for artefactual peaks, per the false-peak preprint ([bioRxiv 2024.06.17.599025](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.06.17.599025.full.pdf)).
3. Run the README simulation step with ms using your Ne, mu and generation time, from 0.3 to 3 Myr, and report the 1.5x-10x interval, not a point. Include simulations with low migration (0.1 migrant per generation) to see whether gene flow would reproduce the 0.7-0.85 Myr spike.
4. Remove X contigs (synteny to the trizona chromosomes, or half-autosomal depth), plus contigs under about 100 kb, and rerun. Report how much sequence this removes.
5. Test sensitivity: mask the betulina ROH and rerun; mask repeats (Delphinoidea authors saw a small effect from repeat masking) **[summary]**; swap the mapping reference if a second assembly is available; compare ONT-derived and HiFi-derived haploid calls for betulina if reads exist.
6. Resolve the WGA question from the sequencing records, and check whether the trizona haploid calls have unusual false-difference or chimera signatures.
7. Run MSMC2 or MSMC-IM cross-coalescence if phasing and sample numbers allow. With one genome per species it will not be informative; state that limit.
8. Check whether BPP was run with a gene-flow model (IM or MSC-I) and what mu and generation-time assumptions were used for its tau.

## Conclusion

The three numbers answer different questions. 2.6 Ma is a fossil-informed prior on an older node, BPP estimates species divergence under its assumptions, and hPSMC reports when cross-lineage coalescence becomes possible. A 0.6-0.7 hPSMC/BPP ratio is what ongoing early gene flow would produce, but it is equally compatible with a wrong mu or generation time, a large ancestral Ne, or data artefacts. Only the simulation-bracketing step and the sensitivity runs above can separate these.

The larger gap is in the literature. There is no published hPSMC test on human splits, no simulation above 5 Ma, no hPSMC-vs-MSC comparison, and no quantified effect of ONT, ROH or WGA. A Sicista result therefore has to carry its own simulations as validation.
