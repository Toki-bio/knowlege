Summary

The split internal promoter of RNA polymerase III is two short sequences inside the transcribed region. The A box sits around tRNA positions 8–19 and the B box around 52–62. The current mechanistic picture is settled and strikingly asymmetric. The B box is a high-affinity, base-read anchor for the τB half of TFIIIC: one base change can cost 56- to 370-fold in binding. The A box is a low-affinity element read mainly by DNA shape, bound by τA through a long flexible linker. That A-box contact places TFIIIB and fixes the start site. This was proposed in 1982 and confirmed biochemically by 1987. Cryo-EM structures in 2023 and 2025 made it visible (Hall et al. 1982; Seifert-Dávila et al. 2025). In SINEs, an intact box pair is necessary but far from sufficient. TFIIIC sits on hundreds to thousands of Alus. Pol III occupies only tens of the roughly 1.2 million copies, and transcripts are detectable from only about 1,300. The regulated step is the TFIIIC→TFIIIB hand-off, which chromatin repressors such as ChAHP block (Oler et al. 2010; Conti et al. 2015; Schnabl-Baumgartner et al. 2026). Upstream flank, nucleotide context and the 3′ end add further layers, so box quality alone cannot rank promoter strength. A new scan of 229 SINEBase consensi, run for this report, puts the asymmetry in numbers. 88 % keep a tRNA-grade B box but only 69 % keep a tRNA-grade A box, and tRNA-headed consensi reproduce the intron-less tRNA spacing almost exactly. The 7SL-type heads (Alu, B1) carry an atypical A box that a tRNA-trained matrix scores below chance. The ID B box breaks the one invariant A that Martignetti and Brosius flagged in 1995. The report closes with three lists: a timeline, the older works to look for in a library, and every limitation of access or computing that left a direction unfinished.

The current consensus: two boxes doing two different jobs

The modern model has three layers, each with direct evidence. Recognition is B-box-driven. Human τB clamps the B box with several winged-helix domains. It makes base-specific contacts to G66, T68, C69 and G70 on the non-template strand and G69, G74 and G75 on the template strand, through R401, R422 (WH3) and K657 (eWH), and it also widens and compacts the grooves. That is combined sequence and shape readout at low-nanomolar affinity, on the B box GGTTCGAGTCC of human TRR-TCT3-2 (Seifert-Dávila et al. 2023). The yeast 2.46 Å τB–DNA structure assigns the matching contacts to τ138 R139, S140, H162, K223, R366, K370, K400, D591 and K593. In single-molecule work, B-box mutation almost abolished binding, while mutating four conserved A-box bases did not stop stable TFIIIC binding (Seifert-Dávila et al. 2025). This is the same answer equilibrium binding gave in 1986. B-box mutations away from consensus cut the binding constant 43- to 370-fold, A-box point mutations cut it less than 2-fold, and deleting the whole A box cut it 2- to 5-fold (Baker, Gabrielsen & Hall 1986).

Positioning is A-box-driven. In the first A-box-engaged τA structure, τ95 and τ131 loops sit in the A-box minor groove. The contacts are mostly to the backbone, and "no more precise register of DNA sequence recognition" could be assigned. The authors therefore propose shape readout. Footprinting shows the A box is what TFIIIB needs: without it there is no TATA-like protection and no TFIIIB-induced bend (Seifert-Dávila et al. 2025). Spacing tolerance has a structural explanation: a ~550-residue flexible linker in human TFIIIC220 (~100 residues in yeast) lets τA "fly-cast" for an A box 30–67 bp from the anchored τB (Seifert-Dávila et al. 2023). Natural yeast tRNA genes span 31–93 bp A–B spacing (Seifert-Dávila et al. 2025).

Activity is chromatin- and context-limited, not box-limited, and this matters most for SINEs. In HeLa, 13 of 1,099,242 Alu dimers, 2 of 90,882 Alu monomers and 1 of 587,443 MIRs were Pol III-occupied (Oler et al. 2010). ENCODE RNA-seq from seven cell lines found ~1,300 Alu loci with Pol III-type transcripts (Conti et al. 2015). TFIIIC peaks over Alus, by contrast, rose from 140 to 3,096 on serum starvation, with no BDP1 or RPC39 recruitment (Ferrari et al. preprint). In mouse ES cells, ChAHP silences B2 SINEs "by blocking TFIIIB recruitment without affecting TFIIIC binding", and DNA methylation plays only a minor role (Schnabl-Baumgartner et al. 2026). Methylated SINE DNA is still Pol III-occupied, while inhibiting histone methyltransferases releases SINE expression (Varshney et al. 2015). Mouse B2 is the exception in scale. Direct sequencing of Pol III transcripts found 16,000–20,000 transcribed B2 copies per cell type (Borodulina et al. 2025).

Around the boxes, three further cis layers are now documented. First, a TATA-like box at −31/−24, supplied by the genomic flank at each insertion, measurably tunes B1, B2, 4.5SH and 4.5SI transcription (Tatosyan et al. 2020). Second, nucleotide context outside the boxes matters. The 4.5SI promoter is 12× stronger than 4.5SH, yet moving all three of its boxes into 4.5SH lowered activity by 40 % (Stasenko et al. 2023). Third, a 3′ module controls the stability of Pol III SINE RNAs through AAUAAA-dependent polyadenylation. It comprises AATAAA, the terminator, a β signal immediately downstream of the B box and a τ signal (Borodulina et al. 2016; Ustyantsev et al. 2021). No structure of TFIIIC on a SINE template could be located; every structure uses tRNA or 5S DNA.

The names and the strings came from four laboratories in 1981–1982

The terms were coined almost simultaneously. "Split promoter" first appears as a title in Hofstetter, Kressmann and Birnstiel (May 1981) (PubMed). "A and B blocks" are credited to Galli et al. 1981 by Hall, Clarkson and Tocchini-Valentini, who wrote "These sequences, termed the A and B blocks (Galli et al., op. cit.), have the approximate coordinates 8–19 and 52–62". Later papers nevertheless cite the minireview for the terms (Hall et al. 1982; Stillman & Geiduschek 1984). "Box A/Box B" is the Cortese group's usage. They called the parts "region a/b/c" in February 1982, then "Box A and Box B" in the first sentence of Dente et al. 1982 (Ciliberto et al. 1982; Dente et al. 1982). "Internal control region" comes from Engelke et al. 1980 for 5S genes and DeFranco, Schmidt and Söll 1980 for tRNA genes. "D-/T-control region" is the Söll group's term (Engelke 1980; DeFranco 1980; Sharp et al. 1982). Whether Galli et al. themselves wrote "block A" or "A block", or used the letters only in a figure, could not be checked. The Nature full text was not reached.

The consensus strings are the most frequently mis-attributed facts in the field. They are given below verbatim, each with the source that printed it. The earliest verbatim printing located is Perez-Stable, Ayres and Shen 1984, citing Galli 1981 and Ciliberto 1983. As OCR'd from the PMC scan, it reads box A "5′ T-G-G-C-N-N-A-G-T-N-G-G 3′" (twelve letters as printed, so the OCR or the original may carry an extra N) and box B "5′ G-G-T-T-C-G-A-N-N-C-C 3′". Box B is "located 33–42 bp downstream from the box A sequence" (Perez-Stable et al. 1984). The attribution of TGGCNNAGTGG / GGTTCGANNCC to Galli 1981 itself appears in later papers but could not be verified against the original.

The only numeric matrices in the open are Pavesi et al. 1994, as re-implemented in tRNAscan-SE's EufindtRNA. The A box is a 21-column matrix over tRNA positions 7–25 with gap handling at 17, 20a and 20b. The B box is an 11-column matrix over 52–62. The cut-offs are B > −14.14, A+B+distance > −31.25 and total > −31.8, with the A–B distance scored from 24 to 66 nt (pavesi.c; eufind_const.h). Read column by column, the matrices give TRGYNNARYGG and GGTTCRANYCC, so they are the quantitative form of the classical strings. One string conflict should stay visible. An earlier pass of these notes rejected the Alu A box TGGCTCACGCC as an unmatched web snippet. A later pass found it printed in Conti et al. 2015, and that source stands. SINEs carry the promoter but rarely use it

The SINE story runs in three waves. The internal promoter of Alu was shown functionally in 1981, before anyone called it tRNA-like (Fuhrman et al. 1981; Elder et al. 1981). Paolella et al. then showed in 1983 that it is "organised in a bipartite structure similar to the split tRNA promoters", with "very little constraint" outside the boxes (Paolella et al. 1983). The 1985 tRNA-origin papers followed. Daniels and Deininger framed them with "Most SINEs contain an intragenic RNA polymerase III promoter that initiates transcription at the 5′ end" (Daniels & Deininger 1985). Okada's lab turned the promoter into a discovery assay. Total genomic DNA transcribed in a HeLa extract yields discrete Pol III RNAs, which led to the tortoise, newt, salmon, squid, octopus and equine SINEs. By 1994, twenty tRNA-derived SINEs had been characterised (Endoh & Okada 1986; Sakagami et al. 1994).

For the four standard controls, the literature and the new scan agree. Alu's promoter lies in the left monomer, with the A box near the 5′ end and the B box around +70 to +82. Its A box is atypical, and the whole promoter was measured as weaker than tRNA-derived galago SINEs: "the Monomer and Type II family promoters are more closely related to the RNA polymerase III promoter consensus sequence than is the Alu family promoter" (Daniels & Deininger 1991). 7SL, the Alu progenitor, needs 37 nt of upstream sequence. Ullu and Weiner inferred that most Alus lack this element, so "only a small subset of such sequences can be transcribed in vivo" (Ullu & Weiner 1985). ID/BC1 has "an A box element and a variant B box element … lacking the invariant A residue found in the consensus B boxes of tRNAs". Its activity depends on a correctly spaced upstream region with TATA and PSE sites, and a consensus tRNA B box replaces it efficiently "only if the BC1 upstream region is removed" (Martignetti & Brosius 1995). B2 "contains regions of homology to the RNA polymerase III split promoter". Its first 101 bp, including the boxes and the β signal, are its most conserved part (Krayev et al. 1982; Vassetzky et al. 2021).

Head type sets the promoter architecture. 5S-derived SINEs (zebrafish SINE3, catfish SINE3_IP, salmonid OS-SINE1, megabat MEG-RS/RL) use the type-1 box A + IE + box C promoter, so no B box is expected (Kapitonov & Jurka 2003; Gogolevsky et al. 2009). AmnSINE1's residual tRNA segment "lacks a Box B sequence", so the 5S part took over as promoter (Nishihara et al. 2006). Extra B boxes arose by insertion or duplication in Dip_a2, Dip_b2 and rodent B1, and Vassetzky et al. suggest an extra B box may let SINEs "bypass cellular mechanisms repressing their retrotransposition" (Vassetzky et al. 2021). In old SINEs such as MIR/Ther-1, the tRNA-like similarity "often remains limited to boxes A and B" once the cloverleaf is lost (Mobile DNA 2019 figure; Gilbert & Labuda 1999).

Why most copies are silent has two pre-2000 answers that were never separated experimentally. The first is intrinsic promoter decay. Methylating "a single pair of CpG dinucleotides in the A box" represses, and three CpG→CpA changes "within and near the A box drastically decrease" template activity (Liu & Schmid 1993). Transcribed Alu cDNAs "exactly match the A box", and selection favours "the consensus sequence in the first 42 bases and the promoter B box" (Liu et al. 1994; Shaikh et al. 1997). The second is insertion context: upstream flanks of Pol III genes raise Alu, B2 and BC1 transcription, and 7SL's flank works best (Roy et al. 2000). For human Alu, deleting the native 5′ flank cut transcription 4- to 5-fold in several loci, and 6 of 9 Alus lost at least 2-fold (Conti et al. 2015). Box integrity does predict occupancy for mouse SINEs. Of the 241 Pol III-bound copies, 87 % carry a B box, 64 % an A box, and 80 % sit in the low-divergence class (Carrière et al. 2012). It does not predict occupancy for human tRNA genes: "Occupied and unoccupied tDNAs showed nearly identical A-box and B-box sequences" (Oler et al. 2010).

No published experiment that mutates the Alu (or B1/B2) A box versus B box and measures retrotransposition quantitatively could be located. Comeaux et al. state explicitly that they did not test box mutations, because mutations in the left monomer would also change transcription (Comeaux et al. 2009). The tool that would remove this confound now exists: Alu RNA driven from an external CMV promoter retrotransposes efficiently. So far it has been used only for deletion mapping, which found a 46-nt 5′ domain (Moldovan et al. 2025).

No published census of box retention across SINE families could be located, so one was computed for this report. Inputs: 229 SINEBase consensi (local copy SINEBase.nr95.uniq.fa, md5 00ba0a8e1b1798526bc8bf7ab97b1fff) and 419 human tRNA genes from GtRNAdb hg19, 29 of them with introns (md5 6b54e95ca5418d7d9c4d331ca3b2c96f). Method: A and B sites in each tRNA gene were seeded with loose regexes, with a fallback to the fewest mismatches to the Galli-type strings TGGCNNAGTGG / GGTTCGANNCC. 11-column log2-odds matrices were built (pseudocount 0.5, uniform background), refined once, and scored leave-one-out on the tRNA genes. Each consensus was scanned on the sense strand for the best A box, the best B box 20–120 nt downstream of it, a head A box within the first 40 nt, and a second pair (dimers). Two thresholds were used. "tRNA-grade" means at or above the 5th percentile of tRNA-gene scores (A 8.97, B 9.15). "Above null" means above the 95th percentile of 20 dinucleotide-preserving shuffles of that consensus (esl-shuffle -d -N 20 --seed 42). Head type came from cmscan -E 1 against three Rfam models: RF00005 tRNA, RF00001 5S_rRNA and RF00017 Metazoa_SRP.

The tRNA reference itself is noisier than the textbook strings suggest. The data-derived consensus is TGGCTCAGTGG for the A box (13.0 bits) and GGTTCGAATCC for the B box (15.8 bits). The strict regex found the A site in only 291 of 419 genes and the B site in 323, so variable D-loop length defeats a fixed 11-mer about 30 % of the time. Against a pooled null of 4,580 shuffles, 22 of 419 real tRNA A boxes and 11 of 419 B boxes do not beat the null's 95th percentile. Start-to-start A→B spacing in tRNA genes has a median of 44 (range 42–79). Intron-less genes span 42–55, with a 53–55 group of long-variable-arm genes. Intron-containing genes span 56–79, median 64 (trna_summary.txt).

Four readings follow. First, the B box outlasts the A box in every head class. This matches the plant result that "SINE B boxes are significantly more highly conserved" (Wenke et al. 2011), the mouse ChIP figures of 87 % vs 64 % (Carrière et al. 2012), and the structural asymmetry that makes the B box the binding anchor. Second, the spacing of tRNA-headed SINEs is the intron-less tRNA spacing: 75 of the 87 pairs fall at 43–45. Apart from single cases at 56–63 and one at 75, no SINE shows the intron-shifted 56–79 spacing. That fits heads derived from processed tRNA transcripts. It also means regex tools capped at a 50-nt spacer lose little on tRNA-type heads. Third, the boxes outlive the Rfam-detectable head. Consensi with no RNA head at E ≤ 1 still carry a tRNA-grade pair in 50 % of cases and both boxes above their own null in 66 %. Fourth, 5S heads score low on both boxes, as a type-1 promoter predicts. The one 5S-labelled exception, SB10, carries a perfect tRNA-type pair at positions 11 and 53, so it is either composite or a weak 5S call. In tRNA-grade sites, mismatches to the Galli strings were 0/1/2/3/4 in 47/65/30/7/8 A boxes and 0/1/2/3 in 64/55/16/5 B boxes.

These controls reproduce three findings from the literature without being tuned to them. The Alu B box AGATCGAGACC contains, at its positions 2–10, the 9-mer GATCGAGAC. That matches Conti's GWTCGAGAC and Murphy and Baralle's active "GTTCGAGAC" at W = A. The Alu and B1 A boxes share the tRNA 5′ half (TGGC) but diverge at the 3′ GTGG, and so fall below chance. This is the "C instead of G" difference Conti describes, and it echoes "SINEs B1 and Alu possess the same atypical boxes A" (Tatosyan et al. 2020). The ID B box GGTTCGGTCCT carries a G where the tRNA consensus has its invariant A (tRNA A58). That is the "variant B box lacking the invariant A residue" Martignetti and Brosius described for BC1 in 1995. B2, the only control with two tRNA-grade boxes, is also the SINE that moves as efficiently as Alu in L1-driven assays, while B1 moves 20–100-fold less. That retrotransposition result is abstract-level only and does not test promoters (Dewannieux & Heidmann 2005).

Three subsets are worth singling out. Seventeen consensi carry an A box without a downstream tRNA-grade B box, mostly with a B 11-mer one or two changes off at an invariant position: SlmII, EC-1, OR1, Bfl1, Oegop, DAS-IIb, B4, Sepioth2A, OR2, PRE-1, Sepioth2B, MEG-T2, SB1, SB4, SB2, ID-Spe and ID. Examples are PRE-1 GATTCAATCCC (8.39) and OR1/OR2 GTTTCGATTCC (8.82). Sixty-two carry a B box without an A box. Twenty-three of these are Rfam-tRNA heads, such as CQ-1 with A TGGCTGACTGG (7.85) and B GGTTCGATGCC, AC1 with A TGGTGTGGTGG (6.43), and Bov-tA with A TGGCTCAGATG (8.95). The rest are all the 7SL-type elements (B1, pB1, Tu-I, Tu-II) and 36 with no Rfam-detected head (A_without_B.tsv; B_without_A.tsv). Two dimers pass the tRNA-grade test for both units. STRIDM has A TGGCTCAGTGG @13 / B GGTTCGATCCT @56 and a second pair, A TGGCTCAAGCG @110 / B GGTTCGATCCT @153, each at spacing 43. IDL-Geo has A @13 / B GGTTCGATTCC @56 and a second pair, A TGGCTCAAGTG @107 / B AGTTCAAGCCC @170, at spacing 63 (dimers.tsv).

Several caveats bound these numbers. The matrices were seeded by Galli-like regexes, so they are partly circular: "tRNA-grade" means "resembles regex-found human tRNA sites". The matrices are trained on human tRNAs and applied to plant, fish and invertebrate consensi. An 11-mer matrix cannot follow the variable-gap A box. Twenty shuffles make each consensus's own null little more than its best shuffle. Rfam at E ≤ 1 with only three models misses decayed heads and cannot see U-snRNA heads. A family consensus can be master-like and overstate what genomic copies retain. Spacing is start-to-start, so the literature's "30–40 bp", measured end-to-start, corresponds to about 41–51 here. Only the sense strand was scanned.

Conflicts the record does not resolve

Which box dominates Alu. In tRNA genes, the A box positions the start and the B box anchors binding (Hall et al. 1982; Baker et al. 1987). In the 3′-α1 Alu, by contrast, the posterior (B) element "alone is sufficient and necessary for accurate initiation", and the distance to the start is constant relative to B, not A (Perez-Stable et al. 1984). Two years later the same group found the start fixed 4–5 bp upstream of box A at spacings of 47–298 bp, and an A-like second element activated at long spacing (Perez-Stable & Shen 1986). Meanwhile the regulatory literature puts Alu's fragility in the A box: methylation sensitivity, CpG decay, the fidelity of transcribed copies, and the nucleosome positioned over it (Liu & Schmid 1993; Liu et al. 1994; Englander & Howard 1995). The B-box mutation Conti tested caused "a dramatic reduction" in Alu transcription (Conti et al. 2015). One reading reconciles all of this: the B box is necessary for binding and the A box is the decay-prone positioning element. No single experiment tests that reading on Alu.

The 1985 tRNA isotype assignments. Sakamoto and Okada assigned rat ID to phenylalanine tRNA, rodent type 2 Alu (≈ B2) to lysine tRNA5, and rabbit C and the bovine/goat 73-bp repeat to glycine tRNA (Sakamoto & Okada 1985). Daniels and Deininger assigned rat ID to alanine and mouse B2 to serine, and the galago Monomer/Type II to methionine (Daniels & Deininger 1985). Later work indexes the BC1/ID phylogeny under tRNA-Ala (Martignetti & Brosius 1993). The scan in this report labels heads only to tRNA/5S/7SL and does not settle the isotypes. That needs whole-molecule isotype models, as the earlier report C:\work\Raks_COI\reports\tRNA similarity scans for SINEs.md describes.

Alemán 2000 versus promoter-quality succession. Daniels and Deininger found promoter strength tracking copy number and recency across three galago SINE families (1991), and Liu and Schmid tied young-Alu success to A-box CpG integrity (1993). Alemán et al., however, found that "Alu subfamily diagnostic mutations do not significantly alter the amount of Alu RNA". Right-half and 3′-flank variation did change RNA levels, so master elements win through "a multitude of individual differences" (Alemán et al. 2000). Comeaux's estimate of a ~3-fold subfamily effect within ~180-fold total regulation sits between the two positions (Comeaux et al. 2009).

Smaller numerical conflicts. The optimal tRNA A–B distance is "about 40–50 nucleotides" in Ciliberto, Traboni and Cortese 1982, but Hall et al. quote the same paper as "30–40 bp" (Ciliberto 1982; Hall 1982). Other values are 34–53 bp (Baker & Hall 1984) and 30–60 bp (Baker et al. 1987). The start-to-A-box distance is "10–16 bp" in Hall 1982 but "about 25 nt" in Marck 2006 (DOI), and the two count from different reference points. The A-block coordinates are 8–13 (Hofstetter), 13–20 (Galli) and 8–19 (Hall). Box integrity predicts occupancy for mouse SINEs (Carrière) but not for tRNA genes (Oler), and Alu in vitro transcription rates did not reflect in vivo expression (Conti et al. 2015).

Conclusion

The forty-five-year arc has a simple shape. The 1982 guess that a factor binds the B block and the A block merely positions the polymerase turned out to be right in detail, down to the residues. What changed is where the action is for SINEs. It is not in recognition, which TFIIIC performs on thousands of Alus, but in the TFIIIB step that the shape-read A box serves. Repressors (ChAHP), viral activators (e1a) and the insertion-site −31/−24 sequence all act at that step. This reframes "promoter decay". A SINE copy can lose activity through B-box erosion (binding), A-box or upstream erosion (positioning), chromatin, or 3′-module decay, and only the first two are visible in a box score.

The new scan turns that into a working rule for SINE annotation. Across 229 families, a tRNA-grade B box is the more robust signature, at 88 % against 69 % for the A box. The boxes persist after Rfam can no longer see the head, so a box pair is good corroboration of a degraded tRNA head. A box score is not a liveness test, though, and for 7SL-type heads a low A score is the expected state, not a defect. The experiment the field still lacks is now technically easy. It would put A-box and B-box point mutants into the CMV-driven Alu or B2 retrotransposition assay and into the 2025 massively parallel jumping library. That would give the first box-to-activity calibration and settle the Alu A-versus-B dispute on its merits.
